### Incident to never repeat

A single 8,961-request batch sat unscheduled for 2 hours. It was cancelled and resubmitted
as 18 chunks, but the cancel raced: the "cancelled" batch had in fact processed
8,960 requests, and they were billed. A second cancel also raced (499 of 500 billed).
Rules from this:

1. **Never cancel a batch to save money.** Cancellation does not stop requests already
   picked up, and you cannot tell how many that is.
2. Submit in chunks of **≤ 500 requests** (`m1.BATCH_CHUNK`); small batches start in minutes.
3. Before a bulk run, submit **one pilot chunk**, read its real token usage, and project
   the full cost from it. The M1 estimate taken from gold-set calls was 2.5× too low.
4. Log spend for every batch that exists, collected or not.

## Cost model, measured in M1

Sonnet 5.5, high effort, anchor-grouped, Batch API: **≈ $0.00053 per middle-band pair**
(≈ 1,800 input + 440 output tokens per request, ≈ 6.8 pairs per request). Input is
≈ 46 % of the cost, output (thinking + JSON) ≈ 54 %.

M2 + M3 add ≈ 19,200 non-empty narrations (Abu Dawud 5,272; Tirmidhi 3,924; Nasa'i 5,679;
Ibn Majah 4,338). At M1's pair density that is ≈ 100k new candidate pairs and ≈ 60k
middle-band pairs: **≈ $32 with the M1 method**. That leaves too little for translation.

### Cheaper method for M2+ (each lever measured on the M1 gold set)

| Lever | Volume saved | Cost on gold |
|---|---|---|
| Middle band with lexical score < 0.32 → no LLM; record as "see also, unconfirmed" | ≈ 30 % of pairs | ≈ 11 % of that band's true merges become see-also links instead (4 of 36 gold pairs) |
| Haiku (medium) pre-filter: drop pairs it calls related/different with confidence ≥ 0.7 | ≈ 25 % of the rest | 0 of 100 true merges lost on gold; Haiku costs ≈ 1/20 of Sonnet |
| Cap prompt texts at 150 words (now 400) | ≈ 10 % | not measured; re-check precision on gold first |

Combined: ≈ 47 % of the M1 method's volume → **≈ $15 for M2 + M3**. Keep Sonnet at **high**
effort for what remains: medium (95.8 %) and low (93.6 %) fall below the 97 % gate on gold.

Translation (M4) then has ≈ $30–35 left: Haiku translation of ≈ 9–10k representatives
(≈ $2–5) fits; Sonnet translation (≈ $30) does not fit beside M2–M3. Run the Haiku vs
Sonnet bake-off on 50 hadith (≈ $0.50) and decide with the owner.

## Clustering as of M1 (keep it)

- Fragment edges form a **forest**: a fragment joins one container, the most specific one.
  A container that would take in two unrelated fragments is a compound and absorbs none
  (212 compounds in M1). Without this, lists of prohibitions chained unrelated hadith
  into a 292-member cluster.
- Large sparse clusters are split into weighted Louvain communities (173 splits).
- Back-references are re-attached to their anchor after every split.
- **Ambiguity tags** (owner's request: don't spend on hard cases, tell the reader):
  a merge that rests on a low-confidence (< 0.7) decision marks the hadith "≈ grouping
  uncertain"; two-Sonnet-run disagreements (1,544 pairs) and low-confidence no-merge calls
  become "see also (possible duplicates, unconfirmed)" links.
- Recall trade-off: only 56 % of Muslim's multi-member FAB groups end up in a single
  hadith; per-book unique counts are above the classical figures (Bukhari 3,505 vs
  ≈ 2,600; Muslim 3,997 vs ≈ 3,033). Muttafaq ʿalayh 1,961 vs ≈ 1,906 in al-Luʾluʾ
  wa-l-Marjān. Precision first was the owner's choice; duplicates remain linked by see-also.
